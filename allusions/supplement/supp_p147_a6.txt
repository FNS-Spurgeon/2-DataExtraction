1896. Garrett, A. C. Studies on Chaucer's House of Fame, [in]
Harvard Studies and Notes in Philology and Literature, Boston,
Mass., 1896 (issued 1897), vol. v, pp. 151-75.
[Concludes, I, that the allegory probably denotes the inner
experience of the poet, the first part of the poem concerning
the Temple of Venus,—the interests of youth; the second
part, the Temple of Fame,—the special interest of mature
life, the winning of a name. II, that the story of Alcestis
was probably to form chief part of the continuation. Discusses
Chaucer's debt to the folk-tales of the Eagle and the Glass
Mountain. See further on this question Studies in Chaucer's
Hous of Fame, by Wilbur Owen Sypherd, Chaucer Society,
1907, and Neilson.]
