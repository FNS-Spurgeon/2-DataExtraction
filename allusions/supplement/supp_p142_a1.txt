1895. Macaulay, G. C. ‘Troilus and Criseyde’ in Prof. Skeat’s
edition, [in] The Academy, vol. xlvii, April 6, 1894, pp. 297, col.
3, 4; 298, col. 1, 2, 3.
[After great praise of the excellence of the text, Macaulay
examines two points : (1) who is 'Lollius'? whom Chaucer
says he mainly follows in 'Troilus,' and (2) was Chaucer, in
this poem, indebted to Guido delle Colonne at all? His
answer to (1) is that it is clear that by 'Lollius' Chaucer
means Boccaccio; and to that for this poem Chaucer drew
from Benôit de Sainte-More and not from Guido.]
