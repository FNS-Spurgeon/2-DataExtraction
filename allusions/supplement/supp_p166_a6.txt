1900. Mead, William E. The Prologue of The Wife of Bath's Tale, [in
Publications of the Modern Language Assn. of America, Baltimore,
1901, vol. xvi (new series, ix), pp. xl, 388-404.
[General sketch of the Wife of Bath likening her to a
compound of Falstaff and Mrs. Caudle. The possible debt of
Chaucer in creating her to La Vielle in the Romaunt is dis¬
cussed. She is one of the earliest shrews in English literature.
and was perhaps introduced among the pilgrims to effect a
balance to the suffering wives of some of the stories (p. 395).
A further probable debt of Chaucer's to the Romaunt (from
I. 9204) and to St. Jerome’s Epistola adversus Iouinianum
(wherein the Aureolus liber de Nuptiis of Theophrastus, used
by Meury, is preserved) is dealt with.]
