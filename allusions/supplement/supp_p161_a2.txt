Chaucer's personages all appeal to and interest us;
Shakspere's, though equally as well drawn as Chaucer's, do
not. The early poet has much sympathy with his characters,
and tries, with no little success, to enlist that of his readers in
[p. 180] them... But Shakspere has no sympathy with his characters,
and awakens none in us. All his characters are much less
worthy of our admiration and respect than Chaucer's are.
Both writers are gifted in characterization, but in a different
way. Shakspeare is analytic; Chaucer is descriptive.
