1900. Gummere, F. B. Is Chaucer to be reckoned as a Modern or
Mediæval Poet? [in] Publications of the Modern Language Assn. of
America, Baltimore, 1901, vol. xvi (new series, ix), pp. xxxvii-xl.
[Judged by his genius, Chaucer belongs to no one period;
judged by the conditions which governed the making of his
poetry, he is mainly mediæval; it is otherwise in his
sentiment and humour. "In the middle ages sentiment and
humour were largely impersonal ; sentiment either lay in
solution with the material of the poem, or else belonged to a
a guild, as in the case of the hymn. Humour, too, was an
affair of communities rather than persons.  ... On the other
hand, both modern humour and modern sentiment are over-
whelmingly individual.  ... Turning to Chaucer, and applying
these two tests, the critic is fain to say that this great poet
is tentatively modern in his sentiment, triumphantly modern
in his humour, and distinctly modern in the attitude which
he takes toward his own work, pp. xxxvii-viii. This is
modified as the paper progresses.]
