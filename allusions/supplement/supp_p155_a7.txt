1898-9. Neilsen, William Allen. The Origins and Sources of the
Court of Love, [in] Harvard Studies and Notes in Philology and
Literature, 1898, vol. ii, pp. i-vi, 1-284, vol. vi, p.  .
[The vol. is substantially a dissertation presented to Harvard
in 1898 for the degree of Doctor of Philosophy. An elaborate
study of the theme, its influence, and sources.]
[p. 289] To sum up. The author of the Court of Love was most
influenced by Chaucer and Lydgate. The poems by the
former which are most used by him are the Legend of Good
Women, Parlement of Foules, Hous of Fame, and Compleynt
to Pity. Troilus and Criseyde is frequently recalled by single
lines, and by such episodes as the swooning of Philogenet.
The Bok of the Duchesse may have suggested the painted
windows near the beginning of the Court of Love, but the
device is common. Occasional verses show a knowledge of
at least some of the Canterbury Tales. The total result gives
the impression of a very devoted disciple.
