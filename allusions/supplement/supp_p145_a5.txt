1895. Unknown. Review (No. III) of The Complete Works of Geoffrey
Chaucer, edited... by Walter W. Skeat, Oxford, vols. iv-vi, [in]
The Nation, New York, vol. Ix, March 28, 1895, pp, 239-40.
[A critical review of the essay on sources of the tales
(vol. iii) stating, that though the discussion is in the main
satisfactory, "still, there are conspicuous inequalities, some
unaccountable omissions, and a few errors." The reasoning
as to the date of the 'Man of Law's Tale' is not very close;
the existence of a verse translation of Innocent's 'De Con¬
temptu Mundi' is confidently asserted on very weak grounds;
in the matter of Ser Giovanni and Il Pecorone Skeat seems
to have disregarded recent Italian research; the vexed question
of Chaucer's relations to Gower is left in an unsatisfactory
condition; a serious mistake is the ascription of the 'Roman
de la Manekine' and Philippe de Reimes; the summary of
the literature of the  'Prioress's Tale' is meagre and out of date;
more could have been made of the notes on the 'Wife of
Bath's Tale.' The text of the 'Canterbury Tales' (vol. iv) is
admirable, accurate and readable. The notes to the 'C.
Tales' suffer from two general faults: (1) an attempt to serve
the tyro and the adept; (2) discursiveness and lack of
condensation.
being "the very worst note ever written on a passage of
Skeat's notes on the Prioress's French is condemned as
Chaucer, as some of Mr. Skeat's come near being the very
best."
Yet in Chaucer annotation Skeat  "has accomplished enough
to justify one in associating his name with that of Tyrwhitt,
and no Chaucerian can ask for higher honor."
The general Introduction (vol. vi) affords a number of
new observations, especially on metre, and the glossary is
admirable.
The Reviewer concludes that Skeat  "may rest secure of
the gratitude of more than one generation."]
