[p. 23]... Spenser, in many respects a greater poet, is also
read; but by fewer. Nobody, I imagine, will deny this.
But what is the reason of it?
[p. 24] The first and chief reason is this–Forms of language
change, but the great art of narrative appeals eternally to
men, and its rules rest on principles older than Homer.
And whatever else may be said of Chaucer, he is a superb
narrator. . . .
