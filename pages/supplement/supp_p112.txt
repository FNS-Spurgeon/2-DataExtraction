1889. Bridges, Robert, Growth of Love, Sonnet IV (Poetical Works of
Robert Bridges, 1898, vol. i, p. 222).
And sounds will gather beauty from their sense,
The very names of things belov’d are dear,
. . . . . . .
Their sons by name, knowing none would be heard
Thus may I think the adopting Muses chose
Or writ so oft in all the world as those,—
Dan Chaucer, mighty Shakespeare, then for third
The classic Milton, and to us arose
Shelley with liquid music in the word.
[This sonnet is not in the first edition of Growth of Love
1876. The printed edition of 1890 varies from the above in
the following two lines: line 1. "... of things we love;"
line 9, "For I must think..."]

Language Notes, Baltimore, 1889, vol. iv, pp. 65-67.
[The article notes Brandl’s  "Uber einige historische An¬
spielungen in den Chaucer-Dichtungen" (Engl. Studien, Heft 2,
Bd. xii); Herzberg (Lemcke’s Jahrb viii, 133); ten Brink
(Ch. Studien, i, 165) ; Skeat (Prioresses T., p. lxxxi.) ; and
gives data on the imperfections in ryme. From the evidence
McChumpha would conclude that Chaucer was not the
author, and that the original dialect of the Dream was not
Northumbrian, but a Midland dialect exhibiting Northumbrian
tendencies.]
