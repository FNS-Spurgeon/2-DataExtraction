1891. Stewart, Hugh Fraser. Boethius, an Essay, pp. 202-6, 214-30,
260-70.
[Containing a study of Chaucer's translation of Boethius.
The author’s conclusions are : (1) that Boece is not the
product of Chaucer's earlier years, but of his maturity, p. 226;
(2) that the translation was the work of one who was no
Latin scholar at all, but who had a running knowledge of the
language, and the sagacity to grasp the meaning of his author,
p. 226; (3) that Chaucer's verse renderings are far more
felicitous than his prose. The author forms the view that
Chaucer’s translation was independent, and not assisted by
the O.F. text, pp. 203-6, and points out that the O.F. version
thought to be Jean de Meung's by Dr. Morris (in his edition
for the E.E. T. So., 1868) is an anonymous version of 1477,
p. 203.]
