[Some notes and corrections of Tolman’s review of this
book, 1890, and under Hempl and Tweedie, 1891, below.]
[p. 135] I am glad to see that in the brief life of Chaucer prefixed
there is no reference to the raptus of Cecilia Chaumpayne,
which has most unpardonably been allowed to stand in
previous editions without explanation, leading students to
suppose that the poet was guilty of abominable crime. I
have no doubt that the offence with which Chaucer was
charged was that known as raptus heredis, or unlawfully
taking an heir or heiress from the custody of his or her
feudal guardian. See the 'Provisions of Merton'....
